# TargetWise 1.0.0

* Targeting Design, Exports: **Registration form (XLSForm)**, a draft KoboToolbox / ODK / MODA form built from the adopted design (enabled once adopted). Consent, household ID, enumerator, the area levels of the registration data as cascading lists, GPS, the head of household as beneficiary (first name, surname, third name if any, sex, date of birth and age, marital status, phone, identity document, optional photo, household size), an optional alternate recipient (names, sex, age, relationship, optional photo), one required question per criterion, and hidden calculations: each criterion's points, the vulnerability score (as TargetWise computes it), eligibility at the adopted threshold, and two vulnerability categories (quartile 1-4 and quintile 1-5 of the score on the design data). In English and French (label and hint columns per language, English by default; area names and criterion labels copied from the data). Formatted like the other exports (styled header, frozen row, filters; an "About this draft" summary sheet). It is a draft to adjust: the only export that is not locked. An "About this draft" sheet says what each part does; check the form in the form builder before use.
* Simpler pages, nothing left out (reports and exports keep every detail):

  * Step 4: settings, figures, error matrix and verdict up front; the charts in one **Explore the threshold** card, two per tab side by side: **How well it separates** (ROC and score by vulnerability), **Where households fall** (score distribution and criteria met), **What drives it** (what drives the score and coverage by criterion, taller for many criteria). The **Score percentiles** button sits at the right of the tabs. Quartiles and quintiles give way to a **Score percentiles** button: pick quartiles, quintiles, deciles or your own percentiles, compute them in the dialog and export them to Excel. The design report now also lists the quartiles and quintiles.
  * Data Quality opens on a **Health check**: one row per check (duplicates, missing answers, outliers, area names, spelling variants, enumerators) with what it found and a Review button that opens the tab.

Templates can be exported to a file and imported, to share a standard report with colleagues or another office (charts and earlier rounds' figures, never the data; `template_json()`, `read_template_json()`).
* The monitoring report's executive summary is a quiet strip of figures with hairline dividers and a small status dot, without boxes or thick borders.
* Implementation errors are now measured first on a **post-targeting survey** (or baseline), before distribution; PDM stays as the least preferred option. Targeting Monitoring's new step 0 draws the **verification sample** from the current selection: households selected and not selected, area by area (households excluded by design left out), sized from a margin of error with the finite-population correction or a fixed number, with a reserve list and a seed, exported read-only (field list, analyst list with groups, design, how to use). Each area then gets its own errors and a **decision before distribution** (proceed, review the list first, or pause the distribution there; areas with fewer than 30 households surveyed in a group are not judged), in the page, the Excel export and the monitoring report (`ptm_draw()`, `ptm_area_decisions()`, `R/utils_ptm.R`).

A last autosave runs when the connection closes, and the page asks the browser not to freeze the tab (a web lock).

* Targeting Design has a third framework: **Welfare measure: proxy means test (PMT)**, following World Bank guidance (Grosh & Baker 1995; Grosh et al. 2008; Del Ninno & Mills 2015). Choose the welfare variable (per household with the household size, or per person) and the poverty line per person or the poorest share; the page checks the data requirements. Yes/no criteria are tested against being poor, and numeric proxies (household size, rooms) can be added. Models: least squares on log welfare per person, or a quantile regression at the poverty rate (Brown, Ravallion & van de Walle 2018), both validated on held-out households (R-squared, errors at the line, predicted-against-measured chart). The card keeps points (100 x coefficient; numeric proxies per unit, missing values at the average), the threshold starts at the poverty line with the predicted welfare it stands for, and the report, the workbook (formula, intercept, line) and Beneficiary Selection carry it.
* PMT, continued: the welfare setup sits on one line and counts only once **Validated** (nothing is computed while the line is typed). Six models: least squares, quantile regression at the poverty rate, ridge and lasso (penalty by 5-fold cross-validation), random forest and XGBoost regressions (their average effects become the points; the model's own held-out R-squared is shown). PMT accuracy in PMT terms everywhere: leakage (inclusion error), under-coverage (exclusion error), coverage of the poor and non-poor, targeting accuracy, targeting differential (Ravallion 2000) and the CGH indicator (Coady, Grosh & Hoddinott 2004), plus R-squared and RMSE. New charts: selection by welfare decile, leakage and under-coverage as the cut-off moves, residuals, and the model comparison (table and error trade-off). The page, the design report ("Proxy means test (PMT) design report") and the Excel workbook (its own guide, accuracy fields, comparison, decile and cut-off tables) speak of poor and non-poor households, leakage and under-coverage.
* The card's JSON export keeps full precision (it rounded points to four digits).
* Sharper satellite maps: two satellite basemaps, Esri World Imagery and World Imagery (Clarity, often a more recent capture), drawn at screen resolution on high-density displays; the maps zoom to level 21, and where Esri has no image that deep the deepest real image is enlarged instead of a grey screen (`add_esri_bases()`).
* Faster imports and pages on large data (300,000 households x 49 columns): import 11 s instead of about 25 s (the multiple-choice detector judges distinct values, 11.5 s -> 0.5 s; the worker hands the table back as Parquet, and fread's dates no longer stop that); the Overview draws in 8 s instead of 11.6 s (fast grouping for its figures, no table copy when the date filter covers every row, the map sends no popups above 20,000 points and builds the clicked household's popup on demand: 14 MB instead of 61 MB); setup guessing 3.2 s -> 0.65 s; the table builder opens in 1.5 s instead of 5.8 s; the chart builder draws at most 20,000 points (WebGL), computes box plots on the server and samples violins, while correlations use every household.
* The waiter's console shows the background worker's steps and R's own messages and warnings as they come (`bg_call()` writes them to a log the waiter reads; `tw_step()` relays messages and warnings).

* Faster on large data. Every core of the machine is used (`tw_cores()`; `TARGETWISE_CORES` caps it): random forest and XGBoost run multithreaded, the tuning grid of the single-threaded models and the 20 stability refits are spread over worker processes (`tw_lapply()`, same results as one core). Logistic fits (the model, stepwise selection, the per-criterion screening tests) run on one row per answer pattern with its count when households count equally (`collapse_rows()`): identical estimates, standard errors and AIC, several times faster (a million households usually share a few thousand patterns).
